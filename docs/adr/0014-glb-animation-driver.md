# GLB-embedded clips are driven by the GLBSceneRoot, rooted on the GLB object itself

A `.glb`/`.gltf` referenced as a **PackedScene** carries its own **GLB-embedded clip**s (`gltf.animations`, surfaced on the loaded `THREE.Object3D.animations`). The loader has already bound them to the node names of the GLB. The **GLBSceneRoot** synthetic render type doubles as the **GLB animation driver**, with no second animation slice. It reuses the selection-driven **Animation transport** (ADR-0012) and the shared `usePlaybackLoop` state machine that also runs the **AnimationPlayer** slice (ADR-0011). It differs in two things only. The *clip source* is ready-made glTF clips, not **GodotAnimation** **Track**s that the previewer parses and builds. The *mixer root* is the loaded GLB object **itself**, with **no Animation root / `root_node` indirection**, because the clips are pre-bound to the node names of the GLB. For Godot parity the transport activates on a **synthesised `GLBAnimationPlayer` child row**, a tree-only selectable node with no parser and no render component: Godot's glTF importer exposes the clips of a model on an AnimationPlayer node *inside* the imported hierarchy, not on the root. The driver logic lives on the GLBSceneRoot component but gates on the `selectedNodePath` of that child.

## Considered options

- **Reuse the AnimationPlayer slice and its Animation-root name binding (ADR-0011).** Rejected. That path resolves `NodePath("Node:property")` **Track**s through `THREE.PropertyBinding` against an **Animation root**. glTF clips arrive bound to the node names of the GLB, with no `root_node` and no NodePath text to resolve. The Animation-root indirection adds a resolution step they do not need and a synthetic root they do not have.
- **Bind the driver to the GLBSceneRoot root row.** Rejected. Godot exposes the clips of a GLB on an AnimationPlayer node *inside* the hierarchy, so the tree synthesises that child and the transport binds to *its* selection, where a Godot user clicks. The root row renders the GLB but does not activate the transport by itself.

## Consequences

- **Pose restoration snapshots the whole subtree.** glTF clips, often skeletal, touch arbitrary bones. Stop or deselect therefore restores the local TRS of every descendant from a snapshot taken when the mixer was built, not the track-derived target set that the AnimationPlayer driver restores. The mixer and snapshot exist only while the driver is the selected one, so a scene full of GLBs builds no mixer or skeleton snapshot for a GLB that is never selected.
- **No `loop_mode` to honour.** Native glTF clips loop by default (`LoopRepeat, Infinity`). They have no Godot `loop_mode`, unlike the value clips of the AnimationPlayer driver.
- Playback starts **stopped** (authored/bind pose), play is user-initiated, and deselection restores the pose. These are the transport semantics of ADR-0012, so at most one driver (GLB or AnimationPlayer) animates the scene at a time.
- Like all playback, GLB animation is non-deterministic over time, so its fixtures stay out of the visual-regression manifest. The default stopped render stays byte-stable.
