# Integer, bit and derivative functions

The built-in functions of Godot's `builtin_func_defs` that read the bits of a value, pack and unpack, count and move bits, give carries and wide products, split a float into its fraction and exponent, and take a derivative. Godot marks most of them `high_end`: its Compatibility renderer refuses each with the message `Built-in function "%s(%s)" is only supported on high-end platforms.` (`shader_language.cpp:3657`), and Forward+ and Mobile pass them to the GPU. GLSL ES 3.0, which WebGL 2 runs, has the bits of a float, the 2x16 packs and the plain derivatives. The transpiler builds each other function from the integer and float operations GLSL ES 3.0 has, to the bits GLSL 4.50 defines.

`fma` and the coarse and fine derivatives have no exact form in GLSL ES 3.0, so the transpiler draws each with its nearest form, on WebGL 2 only: `fma(a, b, c)` as `a * b + c`, and `dFdxCoarse` and `dFdxFine` as `dFdx`, and so on for `dFdy` and `fwidth`. WebGPU has the exact functions, but three's TSL does not expose them. On the test driver every probe of these forms is bit-equal with Godot, so the difference below could not be shown there.

Godot 4.6.3-stable (official), probed on llvmpipe (LLVM 20.1.2). Each table shows at most 8 of a probe's rows, picked to show as many mixes of zero, subnormal, normal, infinite and NaN values as fit. The real-GL test (`pnpm test:shaders:gpu`) checks every row, bit for bit, NaN lanes compared as NaN. Generated by `pnpm godot:shader-language`: do not edit by hand.


## The bits of a float

`floatBitsToInt` and `floatBitsToUint` give the bits of each float lane as an int or a uint, and `intBitsToFloat` and `uintBitsToFloat` give the float of the bits.

The transpiler emits `floatBitsToUint(a)`. Rows: `floatBitsToInt(float)`, `floatBitsToInt(vec2)`, `floatBitsToInt(vec3)`, `floatBitsToInt(vec4)`, `floatBitsToUint(float)`, `floatBitsToUint(vec2)`, `floatBitsToUint(vec3)`, `floatBitsToUint(vec4)`, `intBitsToFloat(int)`, `intBitsToFloat(ivec2)`, `intBitsToFloat(ivec3)`, `intBitsToFloat(ivec4)`, `uintBitsToFloat(uint)`, `uintBitsToFloat(uvec2)`, `uintBitsToFloat(uvec3)`, `uintBitsToFloat(uvec4)`. Godot source: `servers/rendering/shader_language.cpp:2947`.

Every row is native on WebGL 2. WebGPURenderer runs the same nodes and is untested (#1788).

## Pack and unpack

Each pack function writes the lanes of a vector into one uint, as 16-bit halves or as 16-bit or 8-bit fixed-point values, and each unpack function reads them back. GLSL ES 3.0 has no 4x8 forms, so three writes a GLSL function of its own for each.

The transpiler emits `packHalf2x16(a)`. Rows: `packHalf2x16(vec2)`, `packUnorm2x16(vec2)`, `packSnorm2x16(vec2)`, `packUnorm4x8(vec4)`, `packSnorm4x8(vec4)`, `unpackHalf2x16(uint)`, `unpackUnorm2x16(uint)`, `unpackSnorm2x16(uint)`, `unpackUnorm4x8(uint)`, `unpackSnorm4x8(uint)`. Godot source: `servers/rendering/shader_language.cpp:3412`.

Lowered rows:

- `packUnorm4x8(vec4)`: WebGL 2 lowered: three's tsl_packUnorm4x8.
- `packSnorm4x8(vec4)`: WebGL 2 lowered: three's tsl_packSnorm4x8.
- `unpackUnorm4x8(uint)`: WebGL 2 lowered: three's tsl_unpackUnorm4x8.
- `unpackSnorm4x8(uint)`: WebGL 2 lowered: three's tsl_unpackSnorm4x8.

The other rows are native on WebGL 2. WebGPURenderer runs the same nodes and is untested (#1788).

## Bit counts and bit fields

`bitCount` counts the set bits of each lane, `findLSB` and `findMSB` give the index of the lowest and highest set bit or -1 for none, and `findMSB` of a negative int gives its highest clear bit. `bitfieldReverse` reverses the bits, `bitfieldExtract(value, offset, bits)` reads a field of `bits` bits from `offset`, with its sign for an int, and `bitfieldInsert(base, insert, offset, bits)` writes one. GLSL leaves a field past bit 31, or a negative offset or count, undefined, so the transpiler gives no promise for one. Random programs draw an offset and a count of 0 to 16 each, and the probes check the defined edges: the whole word (offset 0, 32 bits), the top bit (offset 31, 1 bit) and an empty field at the top (offset 32, 0 bits).

The transpiler emits `shiftRight(shiftLeft(shiftRight(value, offset), room), room)`. ## Carries and wide products

`uaddCarry(x, y, carry)` gives the wrapped sum and writes 1 to `carry` where it wrapped, and `usubBorrow` does the same for a difference. `umulExtended(x, y, msb, lsb)` and `imulExtended` write the high and the low 32 bits of the 64-bit product, and give nothing, so a program calls them as a statement and reads `msb` and `lsb` after it. The transpiler builds the high half from products of 16-bit halves.

The transpiler emits `add(x, y), lessThan(sum, x)`. Rows: `umulExtended(uint,uint,uint,uint)`, `umulExtended(uvec2,uvec2,uvec2,uvec2)`, `umulExtended(uvec3,uvec3,uvec3,uvec3)`, `umulExtended(uvec4,uvec4,uvec4,uvec4)`, `imulExtended(int,int,int,int)`, `imulExtended(ivec2,ivec2,ivec2,ivec2)`, `imulExtended(ivec3,ivec3,ivec3,ivec3)`, `imulExtended(ivec4,ivec4,ivec4,ivec4)`, `uaddCarry(uint,uint,uint)`, `uaddCarry(uvec2,uvec2,uvec2)`, `uaddCarry(uvec3,uvec3,uvec3)`, `uaddCarry(uvec4,uvec4,uvec4)`, `usubBorrow(uint,uint,uint)`, `usubBorrow(uvec2,uvec2,uvec2)`, `usubBorrow(uvec3,uvec3,uvec3)`, `usubBorrow(uvec4,uvec4,uvec4)`. ## Fused multiply-add

`fma(a, b, c)` gives `a * b + c`. GLSL gives it the precision of `a * b + c`, and a GPU may round the product before the sum or not. GLSL ES 3.0 has no `fma`, so on WebGL 2 the transpiler writes `a * b + c`, which the driver may fuse. Godot's `fma` on the test driver rounds twice, as `a * b + c` does there, so the probe is bit-equal on rows built to tell the two roundings apart.

The transpiler emits `add(mul(a, b), c)`. Rows: `fma(float,float,float)`, `fma(vec2,vec2,vec2)`, `fma(vec3,vec3,vec3)`, `fma(vec4,vec4,vec4)`. Godot source: `servers/rendering/shader_language.cpp:3405`.

Lowered rows:

- `fma(float,float,float)`: WebGL 2 lowered: nearest form a * b + c, which a driver may fuse.
- `fma(vec2,vec2,vec2)`: WebGL 2 lowered: nearest form a * b + c, which a driver may fuse.
- `fma(vec3,vec3,vec3)`: WebGL 2 lowered: nearest form a * b + c, which a driver may fuse.
- `fma(vec4,vec4,vec4)`: WebGL 2 lowered: nearest form a * b + c, which a driver may fuse.

## Fraction and exponent

`frexp(x, exponent)` gives the fraction of `x`, from 0.5 to 1 in size, and writes its exponent, so that `x` is the fraction times 2 to the exponent. `ldexp(x, exponent)` gives `x` times 2 to the exponent. Godot lists the exponent of `ldexp` as an out argument, so it must be a variable, though the call only reads it. GLSL leaves `frexp` of an infinity or a NaN, and an `ldexp` past the float range, undefined. The transpiler splits a subnormal from its bits, so a GPU that reads subnormals as zero still gives the fraction and exponent GLSL defines: 1e-40 is `0.545 * 2^-132`. Godot's test driver gives the unnormalised bits `0x3f0116c2` with exponent 0, a listed difference. `ldexp` gives GLSL's value from -278 to 277: `ldexp(3e38, -253)` is `0x00e1b1e6`, where Godot's test driver gives 0. At -252, -251 and -250 both sides are equal. The inferred cause, not read from Mesa's source, is a split of the exponent into halves that flushes 2^-127. It is a listed difference.

The transpiler emits `uintBitsToFloat(sign | fraction << shift | 0x3f000000)`. Rows: `ldexp(float,int)`, `ldexp(vec2,ivec2)`, `ldexp(vec3,ivec3)`, `ldexp(vec4,ivec4)`, `frexp(float,int)`, `frexp(vec2,ivec2)`, `frexp(vec3,ivec3)`, `frexp(vec4,ivec4)`. Godot source: `servers/rendering/shader_language.cpp:3526`.

Lowered rows:

- `ldexp(float,int)`: WebGL 2 lowered: three products by powers of two.
- `ldexp(vec2,ivec2)`: WebGL 2 lowered: three products by powers of two.
- `ldexp(vec3,ivec3)`: WebGL 2 lowered: three products by powers of two.
- `ldexp(vec4,ivec4)`: WebGL 2 lowered: three products by powers of two.
- `frexp(float,int)`: WebGL 2 lowered: exponent and fraction bits.
- `frexp(vec2,ivec2)`: WebGL 2 lowered: exponent and fraction bits.

## Derivatives

`dFdx` and `dFdy` give the difference of a value between neighbouring pixels across and down the screen, and `fwidth` the sum of their sizes. A GPU computes them for each block of 2 by 2 pixels: a coarse derivative takes one difference for the block, and a fine one a difference for each row or column. GLSL ES 3.0 has only the plain forms, which the GPU runs as either, so on WebGL 2 the coarse and fine forms draw as the plain ones. Godot's driver on the test machine runs coarse as fine, so the probes cannot show it. On a 16 by 16 target of `x * x * (y + 0.37)`, a true coarse `dFdx` in the second row of a block is 1.74 where the fine one is 3.74. A probe draws one row of pixels, so `dFdy` is 0 on both sides. The browser's compiler, ANGLE, writes each derivative as `dFdx * r.x + dFdy * r.y` for its screen rotation (`RewriteDfdy.cpp`), so a -0, infinite or NaN neighbour gives +0 or NaN where Godot gives -0, infinity or 0.

The transpiler emits `dFdx(a)`.
